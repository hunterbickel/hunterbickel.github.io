Service Communications Specialist

MTA New York City Transit

New York, NY | July 2025 – Present

Real-time service communications for subways and buses in the Digital Communications Unit.

Service Monitoring and Communication

My primary role is to monitor subway and bus service in the NYCT Operations Command Center, keeping riders updated so they can make informed travel decisions. I watch for incidents that cause delays and gaps in service, as well as reroutes and detours.

When an incident disrupts service I write digital alerts describing the status in customer-facing language, monitor the situation, and update the messaging as it changes. For significant disruptions I make sure service changes that may be complex to much of the riding public are easy to understand, and include the best available travel alternatives for the affected line or route.

Alerts written in the Operations Command Center, as riders see them in the MTA app.

1:1 Live Chat

The other primary responsibility is answering inquiries over live instant messaging, giving direct assistance to NYCT subway and bus riders.

Riders also report feedback and issues about safety, quality of service, and customer experience. These reports are often time-sensitive: my job is to gather as much information as possible and relay it to the appropriate NYCT operations teams so the issue gets resolved quickly.

Communications Manager

Effective Transit Alliance

New York, NY | October 2024 – April 2025

Managed social media and developed communications strategy for a transit advocacy group.

Social Media Planning and Management

My primary responsibility was running the social accounts. The strategy I developed rested on three pillars:

  • Provide thoughtful context to New York transit news and online discourse — electrification, the EWR AirTrain, system planning and modernization, fares, through-running.
  • Create evergreen content on the topics ETA advocates for — open gangway trains, through-running, signaling, expansion, governance.
  • Promote ETA’s work by breaking complex material from reports into digestible posts.

While I ran the accounts: +200 followers on X/Twitter and +1,000 followers on Bluesky.

High Speed Rail Alliance Webinar Appearance

Using my relationship with the High Speed Rail Alliance, I organized an appearance by several ETA members on their webinar series to talk about their work on Penn Station. The live presentation drew more than 130 viewers, and the recording has since passed 1,300 views on YouTube.

Form Follows Function: Capacity Before Architecture at Penn Station examines the current state of the hub, explores through-running with reference to other countries, and explains ETA’s proposal for it.

European Transit and Urban Planning Work-Study

San José State University

France & Spain | May – June 2025

In summer 2025 I participated in a two-week work-study in France and Spain. We received seminars on various transit and urban planning topics, as well as site visits to large rail stations and urban projects. In the months after the trip, I co-authored a 250-page post-trip recap analysis.

Site studies and journal entries from the trip are collected under Research & Writing.

Learning at 200 mph: Lessons for Better Passenger Rail from France and Spain

At the end of the trip I was approached by our teacher — renowned transportation and urban planner Eric Eidlin — to help write an article recapping our biggest takeaways for the Mineta Transportation Institute, the entity that sponsored our trip.

What was supposed to be a simple recap article turned into a 250-page report. It compiles our study group’s biggest takeaways into eight “lessons” and applies them to the U.S. context, with the goal of laying the groundwork for future research on the trip’s key focus areas.
